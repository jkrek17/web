1. The storm in plain words

ERA5 puts its lowest pressure at 925.5 hPa on 08 Nov 2014 0600Z, near 55.0°N 169.8°E. Its fastest 24 h fall was 66 hPa, ending 08 Nov 2014 0000Z at about 49°N (the bomb threshold there is 21 hPa, so it qualifies as a bomb by that yardstick). It first reached hurricane-force-equivalent gusts (index at least 71.7 kt) on 07 Nov 2014 0600Z near 45.5°N 159.8°E, was at that strength in 5 of its 6-hourly fixes (about 30 h, counting each 6-hourly fix as 6 h), and peaked at a gust index of 80 kt on 08 Nov 2014 0000Z. The tracker only picks it up 30 h before the lowest pressure, so its early development, and the 24 h fall above, are only partly captured. The gust swath maximum anywhere in the window, land included, is 84 kt.

2. Track and wind field
3. How it developed

At its first tracked fix (07 Nov 0000Z, 42.0°N) the low was frontal, cold core (Hart B 72 m, lower-level core −343 m). At its lowest pressure it was warm seclusion (B −27 m, lower-level core +235 m). The first fix with a symmetric warm core (the warm-seclusion signature) is 08 Nov 0000Z, -6 h from the lowest pressure.

How to read the structure numbers. Hart’s B says how lopsided the low is: above about 10 m it is frontal, near zero it is symmetric. The lower-level core number says whether the 900 to 600 hPa layer is cold (negative) or warm (positive). A frontal low with a cold core is the early stage; a warm seclusion has warm air wrapped around the centre. The classes come from a coarse 1.5° grid, so treat them as a guide.

4. What drove it: upper-level support

During its fastest 24 h fall (07 Nov 0000Z to 08 Nov 0000Z) the strongest 250 hPa wind within about 540 nm averaged 173 kt. Upper-level divergence at 300 hPa over the centre averaged +2.5 ×10−5/s (positive means air spreading out aloft, which draws air up from below), and 500 hPa vorticity advection +2.2 ×10−9/s² (positive means cyclonic spin arriving aloft). The Eady growth rate (850–500 hPa), a measure of how favourable the temperature contrast and wind shear are for the storm to grow, averaged 1.08 per day, and the moisture in the air column within about 270 nm averaged 23 kg/m².

5. Low-level structure: fronts and the low-level jet
6. Where the strongest wind was

The ocean area with gusts of 71.7 kt or more within 600 nm peaked at 64,653 nm² at 08 Nov 0000Z (-6 h from the lowest pressure). Some such wind was present in 10 of the 25 3-hourly fields (about 30 h). The single highest ocean gust in a 3-hourly field was 84 kt at 07 Nov 0300Z, 338 nm behind and 143 nm to the right of the centre. Of the 71.7 kt+ cells, 66% sat in the rear right quarter of the storm (frame fixed to the direction of motion; where the low was stalled the last direction it moved was used).

Read with care

ERA5 proxy throughout; never validated for storms before the archive begins. The structure measures (Hart’s cyclone phase space) use seven pressure levels on a 1.5° grid, coarser than the original method, so read the structure class as a guide, not an exact classification. Over Greenland and high ground ERA5’s sea-level pressure is extrapolated, and gusts near coasts and sea ice are less reliable. The storm-relative frame uses the last direction of motion while the low is stalled. A storm is one case: nothing here is a trend or a forecast rule.
