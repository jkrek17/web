1. The storm in plain words

ERA5 puts its lowest pressure at 929.4 hPa on 05 Jan 1983 1800Z, near 63.0°N 21.8°W. Its fastest 24 h fall was 49 hPa, ending 05 Jan 1983 1200Z at about 56°N (the bomb threshold there is 23 hPa, so it qualifies as a bomb by that yardstick). It first reached hurricane-force-equivalent gusts (index at least 71.7 kt) on 05 Jan 1983 0000Z near 56.2°N 31.8°W, was at that strength in 5 of its 6-hourly fixes (about 30 h, counting each 6-hourly fix as 6 h), and peaked at a gust index of 92 kt on 05 Jan 1983 1200Z. The gust swath maximum anywhere in the window, land included, is 95 kt.

2. Track and wind field
3. How it developed

At its first tracked fix (03 Jan 0600Z, 44.8°N) the low was frontal, cold core (Hart B 127 m, lower-level core −606 m). At its lowest pressure it was warm seclusion (B 0 m, lower-level core +153 m). The first fix with a symmetric warm core (the warm-seclusion signature) is 05 Jan 1800Z, +0 h from the lowest pressure.

How to read the structure numbers. Hart’s B says how lopsided the low is: above about 10 m it is frontal, near zero it is symmetric. The lower-level core number says whether the 900 to 600 hPa layer is cold (negative) or warm (positive). A frontal low with a cold core is the early stage; a warm seclusion has warm air wrapped around the centre. The classes come from a coarse 1.5° grid, so treat them as a guide.

4. What drove it: upper-level support

During its fastest 24 h fall (04 Jan 1200Z to 05 Jan 1200Z) the strongest 250 hPa wind within about 540 nm averaged 189 kt. Upper-level divergence at 300 hPa over the centre averaged +1.3 ×10−5/s (positive means air spreading out aloft, which draws air up from below), and 500 hPa vorticity advection +3.9 ×10−9/s² (positive means cyclonic spin arriving aloft). The Eady growth rate (850–500 hPa), a measure of how favourable the temperature contrast and wind shear are for the storm to grow, averaged 1.81 per day, and the moisture in the air column within about 270 nm averaged 6 kg/m².

5. Low-level structure: fronts and the low-level jet
6. Where the strongest wind was

The ocean area with gusts of 71.7 kt or more within 600 nm peaked at 36,600 nm² at 05 Jan 1500Z (-3 h from the lowest pressure). Some such wind was present in 11 of the 25 3-hourly fields (about 33 h). The single highest ocean gust in a 3-hourly field was 95 kt at 05 Jan 0900Z, 75 nm behind and 95 nm to the right of the centre. Of the 71.7 kt+ cells, 60% sat in the rear right quarter of the storm (frame fixed to the direction of motion; where the low was stalled the last direction it moved was used).

Read with care

ERA5 proxy throughout; never validated for storms before the archive begins. The structure measures (Hart’s cyclone phase space) use seven pressure levels on a 1.5° grid, coarser than the original method, so read the structure class as a guide, not an exact classification. Over Greenland and high ground ERA5’s sea-level pressure is extrapolated, and gusts near coasts and sea ice are less reliable. The storm-relative frame uses the last direction of motion while the low is stalled. A storm is one case: nothing here is a trend or a forecast rule.
