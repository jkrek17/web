Philadelphia Phillies · 2026-27 offseason

The full plan: +10 wins.

The 2026 Phillies won 88 games but played like an 83-win team (713 runs scored, 698 allowed). This plan fills right field, third base, catcher and the bullpen, moves Trea Turner to center field and Bryson Stott to shortstop, and keeps Aidan Miller as the 2028 infielder. Every move is designed to land before the labor deal expires on December 1.

In priority order

The moves

Scarce pieces first (a right-handed right fielder, a third baseman, a catcher who hits), fungible ones last. Each trade lists why the other club says yes.

  1. 1

    Hire the manager

    Before free agency opens. Alex Cora is reported as the presumed top target; Don Mattingly (79-55 as interim) wants the job. Free agents and their agents want to know who they are playing for, and the window is four weeks.

    OctoberNo lockout effect
  2. 2

    SignSeiya Suzuki, right field

    3 years, $75–80M, plus a club option. The best two-way fit on the market: a .345 projected wOBA, a 22.5% chase rate that attacks the 2026 offense's weakest area, +8 fielding runs in right with a strong arm, and a right-handed bat. He replaces a 2026 right-field group that projected around .300. He will carry a qualifying offer, so he costs the Phillies' 2nd- and 5th-highest 2027 picks.

    ~$26Mper year · QO picksabout +20 runs
  3. 3

    TradeIsaac Paredes, third base, from Houston

    Phillies give Bryan Rincon (SS, top-100 prospect); add RHP Moisés Chace only if needed. Houston says yes because it sheds his $13.35M club option and gets a shortstop to cover Jeremy Peña's free agency after 2027, the same pattern as its Tucker and Bregman decisions. Paredes projects at .340 (his 2026 home/road split says the Crawford Boxes helped; Citizens Bank Park is the 5th-friendliest park for right-handed homers) and is about +9 fielding runs better than Bohm at third. Close after the mid-November MVP vote, which can turn his option mutual. Fallback: Bohm, 1 year at about $9M; Plan C: Teruaki Sato from Japan.

    $13.35M1 year · costs Rinconabout +24 runs
  4. 4

    SignRyan Jeffers, catcher

    3 years, $45–51M. Catcher was the biggest production hole: Realmuto hit .276 wOBA at 36. Jeffers projects at .320–.325 after a hamate fracture, and Realmuto catches 50–60 games as the backup and right-handed bench bat. Jeffers will likely carry a qualifying offer (3rd- and 6th-highest picks) and is a Boras client, so have a contingency done by November 25: Shea Langeliers from the Athletics for a package without Gage Wood (Chace, Alirio Ferrebus, Escobar or Nori; the A's want pitching), or Carson Kelly at 2 years, $15M.

    ~$17Mper year · QO picksabout +7 runs net of defense
  5. 5

    MoveTurner to center, Stott to short; SignGleyber Torres, second base

    Torres: 1 year plus an option, about $15M. Turner's 29.9 ft/s sprint speed beats Justin Crawford's, and his −8.7 fielding runs at shortstop came mostly from lost range. Stott was +4.1 at second and +1.9 at third. The switch lets a real bat play second: Torres's 18.9% chase rate is the lowest of the options. Crawford (.270 against lefties) goes to Triple-A or the bench to keep developing at 23. Torres is the bridge to Aidan Miller, so the deal stays short. Needs Turner's buy-in.

    ~$15M1 year + optionabout +14 runs (+18 bat, −4 glove)
  6. 6

    SignBullpen: Bednar, Vesia, Mayza

    David Bednar 3 years, $39M; Alex Vesia 2 years, $18M; re-sign Tim Mayza 1 year, $4M. Alvarado (lost 2 mph, 11.4% walks), Banks and Raley combined for about 150 innings of 5.90 ERA. Two arms behind Jhoan Duran, not one, because Brad Keller is out after Tommy John surgery. Promote Luke Bowlan (97.1 mph fastball) and McFarlane into leverage roles; non-tender Banks. Adrián Morejón replaces Bednar only on three years and only without a qualifying offer.

    ~$26Mper year combinedabout +20 runs
  7. 7

    SignJake Bauers, left field vs right-handers; TradeBrandon Marsh

    Bauers: 1 year, $8–10M, plus a club option. A real step forward in 2026 (13.9% barrels, 21.7% chase) but one strong year after six weak ones, so project .335 and don't give two guaranteed years. He plays left against right-handers; Derek Hill (+2.9 fielding runs, about $1.2M) plays left against lefties. Then trade Marsh, a $7–8M fourth outfielder in his final arbitration year, for a middle reliever or salary room. The other club gets a lefty-hitting outfielder with speed and a year of control.

    ~$9MMarsh saves ~$7.5Mplatoon bat
  8. 8

    SignA veteran starter and Edmundo Sosa

    One starter on a 1-year deal at $8–10M (Nick Martinez, Kyle Freeland, or Brandon Woodruff as an incentive flier) plus Nestor Cortes on a minor-league deal: starters six through eight cost about 15 runs in 2026. Re-sign Sosa, 1 year plus an option at about $4.5M, as the utility infielder (+2.1 fielding runs at third).

    ~$13Mcombinedabout +8 runs
  9. 9

    Housekeeping

    Non-tender Banks, Garrett Stubbs and Rojas. Add Aidan Miller and Escobar to the 40-man roster before the Rule 5 protection deadline (Rincon too if the Houston deal falls through). Open extension talks with Duran, who becomes a free agent after 2027. Two coaching projects with no transaction: Harper's throw-receiving at first base (−9.7 fielding runs, worst in baseball) and Aaron Nola's fastball and sinker, which lost about 30 runs combined.

The payoff

Where the wins come from

Measured against the 2026 team's true talent, not its 88-win record: by runs scored and allowed it was an 83-win team, and about five of its wins were sequencing luck. Bars show the expected gain; whiskers show one standard deviation of projection error.

Runs gained per 162 games. Combined error is about ±30 runs; season-to-season luck adds roughly ±6 wins on top.

+105runs, about +10 wins: from an 83-win base to roughly 93, with a likely range of 90–96 before luck. The lineup alone goes from 4.58 to 5.01 runs per nine innings.

For comparison: the advisor's version without the Turner/Stott/Torres move is about +88 runs; the lean plan is about +50 (+5 wins). The retool alternative keeps Harper and Schwarber, trades Nola for a young center fielder if the price is right, keeps every prospect, and lands near 90 wins at about $345M.

The clock

Four weeks before the freeze

The collective bargaining agreement expires December 1 and a lockout is widely expected, which would freeze trades, signings and 40-man moves. In 2021, $1.4 billion was signed on December 1 alone.

  1. Now to end of World Series

    Hire the manager. Ownership sets the payroll number. Agree trade frameworks with Houston (Paredes) and the Athletics (Langeliers as the catcher fallback). Line up Bohm and Carson Kelly as fallbacks.

  2. 5 days after the Series

    Exclusive window: re-sign Mayza and Sosa. Houston must exercise Paredes's option; qualifying offers go out (Suzuki and Jeffers expected).

  3. Early to mid November

    Offer Suzuki on day one of open free agency. Sign Bauers and the veteran starter. MVP voting (around November 13) settles Paredes's option clause; close the trade. Qualifying-offer and Rule 5 protection deadlines: add Miller and Escobar.

  4. November 19–25

    Catcher decision point: if Jeffers's agent is waiting out the lockout, execute Langeliers or Kelly by November 25. Sign Bednar and Vesia, and Torres. Trade Marsh once Bauers is in.

  5. November 30

    Tender deadline: non-tender Banks, Stubbs and Rojas.

  6. December 1

    Freeze. Anything left (a bench bat, the Marsh trade) moves to a compressed spring window, where prices rose in 2022.

The next shortstop

Where Aidan Miller comes in

Miller is 22, MLB Pipeline's No. 50 prospect and the system's top position player. A back procedure (radiofrequency ablation on June 5) cost him most of 2026; he returned September 5 at Double-A. He is not the Opening Day answer, but he is the reason second base gets a one-year bridge and not a three-year contract.

November 2026

Protect him

Add him to the 40-man roster before the Rule 5 deadline. Do not trade him while his value is at its low point.

Spring 2027

Prove the back

Double-A or Triple-A, playing every day at shortstop and second.

Mid-2027

Take second base

If he hits, he replaces Torres, who moves to a bench and platoon role or is traded at the deadline.

2028

Shortstop or third

Stott becomes a free agent after 2027 and Paredes is a one-year rental. Either extend Stott at short and play Miller at third, or let Stott go and make Miller the shortstop. Decide by July 2027.

Also ending after 2027: Wheeler's contract and Duran's team control. Realmuto is signed through 2028.

What could go wrong

Risks

The money

About $235M of new cash for 2027 and a record tax bill. If ownership says "flat," run the lean plan (Suzuki, Paredes, one reliever, Mayza, Sosa: about +5 wins) and decide by November 1 rather than chase both.

The lockout and Boras

Jeffers may not sign before December 1. The catcher fallback has to be executed by November 25, not just discussed.

An aging core

Harper, Schwarber and Turner are 34, Realmuto 36, Wheeler 37. The projections include aging, but the downside tail is wider than any upside on the additions.

Turner in center

Elite speed, but he would be relearning the outfield at 34. The plan assumes −5 to +3 runs there; if it fails, Hill and Crawford cover center and Turner plays second.

Paredes away from Houston

His 2026 power leaned on the Crawford Boxes. Citizens Bank Park helps right-handed pull hitters too, but the projection is already cut to .340.

A thinner farm

After Rincon, the system is Gage Wood, Miller (back), Renteria and Spangler. Spend Rincon once, keep Wood and Painter for the 2028 rotation without Wheeler.

Transparency

How this was built

Research. Five research agents covered the Phillies' contracts and farm system, the free-agent market, the trade market (with each trade checked from the other club's side), Statcast valuations of every MLB hitter and pitcher, and defense, baserunning and pitch quality from Baseball Savant. Facts are as reported through October 2, 2026; projected contracts are estimates.

Review. A separate advisor reviewed the draft. It cut the expected offensive gain roughly in half by adding aging, multi-season regression and park effects; it also dropped a reliever trade Seattle would not accept and corrected the qualifying-offer count.

Run values. Lineups are scored with the same exact run-expectancy model as the batting-order study, using projected wOBA cut for age and park: Suzuki .345, Paredes .340, Bauers .335, Torres .330, Jeffers .325, Harper .355, Schwarber .352, Turner .318. Defense uses 2026 Statcast fielding run value; pitching uses regressed rates allowed. Ten runs is about one win.

Limits. Turner's center-field defense and Stott's shortstop defense are estimates, not measured. Contract values, qualifying offers and Houston's willingness are reported or inferred, not confirmed. Any of the free agents may sign elsewhere; each move lists its fallback.
