Philadelphia Phillies · 2026-27 offseason · alternative plan

Keep the core. Get younger around it.

The full plan buys about 10 wins with a record payroll. This alternative keeps Bryce Harper and Kyle Schwarber, because no club offered young talent worth their bats. It trades Aaron Nola for a 27-year-old center fielder if the price is right, gives everyday jobs to Justin Crawford, Andrew Painter and, by midseason, Aidan Miller, and keeps every top prospect. It lands about three wins short of the full plan for about $40M less in 2027 payroll, and sets up the real sell-high decisions for the July deadline.

What the market offered

Why Harper and Schwarber stay

We shopped both stars to Baltimore, the Yankees and the Red Sox. Every offer needed the Phillies to keep paying a large share of the salary, and none brought back a young player clearly worth a 45-homer bat.

The payoff

Where the wins come from

Runs gained over 162 games against the 2026 team's true talent, with the Nola trade done. The retool scores fewer runs than the full plan but fields better.

Full planRetool

Combined error is about ±30 runs; season luck adds about ±6 wins on top.

+72runs, about +7 wins: from an 83-win base to about 90 (87–93). Without the Nola trade, about +59 runs and 89 wins. The full plan is +105 runs and about 93.

The gap to the full plan is mostly offense: no Paredes, Jeffers or Torres, and Bohm instead of a new third baseman. In exchange, the retool keeps Rincon and both later draft picks, and spends about $40M less.

The longer view

July and beyond

This plan gets younger in steps rather than in one winter, because the stars' trade value right now is too low to sell.

Winter 2026

Young regulars

Crawford in left, Painter in the rotation, Cowser in center if the Nola deal lands. Miller and Rincon protected.

Mid-2027

Miller arrives

Takes second or third base if his back holds up; Gage Wood joins the rotation.

July 2027

The fork

Contending: extend Duran or Stott and add. Under .500: sell Duran, Stott and Marsh, each a one-year rental needing no consent, and revisit Turner after a better half raises his value.

2028

The turnover

Wheeler, Stott, Duran and Bohm come off the books. The core becomes Harper, Schwarber, Suzuki, Luzardo and Sánchez plus Miller, Crawford, Painter, Wood and Cowser.

What could go wrong

Risks

An aging core stays

Harper, Schwarber and Turner are 34, Wheeler 37. The projections include aging, but the downside tail is wide and this plan doesn't reduce it in 2027.

Third base is a stopgap

Bohm projects at about average with a below-average glove. If Miller isn't ready by midseason, third base stays a weak spot all year.

The Nola trade may not happen

It needs Nola's consent and Baltimore to pay something for a league-average starter on a long deal. Without it, center field stays with Crawford.

Young players may not be ready

Crawford hit .270 against lefties and Painter had a 5.40 ERA as a rookie. Giving them jobs is a bet on development.

The 2028 rotation

Wheeler retires after 2027. Sánchez, Luzardo, Painter and Wood need one more arm; the money this plan saves should be earmarked for it.

The lockout

Everything is designed to land before December 1. A long lockout shortens the season and lowers the value of one-year deals.

Transparency

How this was built

Research. Agents shopped Harper, Schwarber, Turner, Nola, Realmuto and Wheeler to every plausible buyer, then went deeper on the Yankees and Red Sox, and valued every young MLB-ready player at the Phillies' weak positions with our Statcast model. An advisor reviewed the first draft of this alternative, which sold Schwarber; that version was dropped because no offer clearly beat keeping him.

Basis. Same run model, aging and park adjustments, and 83-win true-talent baseline as the full plan and the 2026 lineup study. Cowser projects at .321 wOBA and Bohm at .318. Contract values and trade odds are estimates as of October 3, 2026; no talks have been reported for any of these players.
